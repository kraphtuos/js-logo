How it works

A ringcode spells a short word with concentric rings, one letter per ring, read from the inside out. Each ring has a single gap, and the gap points at its letter on a dial of 26 slots, with A at 12 o'clock and the rest running clockwise.

The format comes from the Jane Street logo, which is the three-ring code for JSC, Jane Street Capital. Ring sizes, thicknesses and gap widths follow the logo.

The scanner finds rings by their shape, corrects for camera tilt, and snaps every gap to the nearest slot. Like reading a clock, it has to know which way is up. It assumes the code is upright; if it isn't, turn the reading with the orientation controls or pick from all 26 possible readings.

Everything runs in your browser in Rust compiled to WebAssembly. Images and camera frames never leave your device.
